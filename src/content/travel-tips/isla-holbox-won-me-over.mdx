Isla Holbox kept showing up on TikTok and in honeymoon inquiries, so I booked a few nights and went to find out whether it lives up to the hype.

Short version: it does, for the right traveler. This is the part I can't get from a brochure, so this is the part I'm going to tell you about. If you want the full play-by-play on flights, the ferry, and transfers, that all lives in my [Isla Holbox travel guide](/destinations/isla-holbox-a-travel-guide-to-mexico-s-no-cars-fly-and-flop-island). Here I want to talk about what it's actually like once you're there, and whether I'd send you.

## **One Piece of Logistics Advice, Then I'll Move On**

Getting to Holbox is a relay. Plane, then van, then ferry, then a golf-cart taxi, because there are no cars on the island. It sounds like a lot. It isn't, if you book it right.

Book the whole transfer as one package through your hotel. When we stepped off the ferry, our taxi driver already knew exactly who we were, because the driver from the van had texted him our photo. That is the difference between arriving cared for and arriving confused in the heat with your luggage. You can save a little by piecing it together on a big shared shuttle bus, but you'll be on a big shared shuttle bus. I'll pay to skip that every time, and I tell my clients the same. That's the whole logistics lecture. Now the good part.

## **The Two Hotels, and Which One I'd Actually Book You Into**

I stayed at two properties on purpose, so I could compare them with my own eyes instead of guessing off a website.

The first was Ser Casasandra, and it's one of the coolest places I've ever stayed. Our concierge, Gary, was waiting when we pulled up, knew both our first and last names, handed us a cold towel, and walked us in. We got a complimentary upgrade. The tour of the property took five minutes, because there are only 18 rooms. That's boutique in the truest sense. Breakfast is a real menu you order from, not a sad buffet. The mini bar was actually included, espresso machine and all. And the beach on that side is the good stuff: soft, almost kinetic sand, clear water, no seaweed while I was there, and sunsets that made me put my phone down. There is only one all-inclusive on this island, but there are little beach stalls where a smash burger, fries, and a beer run about ten bucks, and a bodega on every corner, so it never felt like a gouge.

The second was Awa, on the far side of the island, and I'm glad I saw it so I can be honest with you. It's beautiful to look at, all modern stone and concrete. But there's essentially no beach, you walk into the water off a pier, and that side was catching a heavy load of seaweed. Service wasn't at the same level. Breakfast wasn't included. Our room wasn't ready until 3:30 when check-in was 3:00, with nowhere indoors to wait while I'm holding a laptop. The pool is shared even if you pay up for a swim-out.

My verdict: take it from me and stay on the Casasandra side. For every dollar you pay you're getting a better experience there.

## **The Whale Sharks: The Best Thing I Did, and the Hardest**

This was the headline excursion, and it was amazing, but I'm not sure I'd do it more than once.

The trip out to the whale sharks was about three hours, far enough that we passed a Carnival cruise ship on the way. That's how deep into the open ocean you are. The water was rough the day we went, and about ninety minutes in the guide asked if we wanted to turn back. We were already committed, so, obviously, no.

When we got there I slipped in and there was a baby whale shark right next to me, the size of my whole body. I went back a second time and saw what they call a Big Mama. I have to tell you, I've traveled a lot and seen a lot of things, but that experience was top of the list.

That said, the trip also wrecked me. The chop got me four separate times. I lost the coffee and the pastry I had that morning straight into the sea. When it was my turn for the second swim, everyone assumed I'd sit it out. But I did not sit it out. I came for a good time and I know how to rally.

So would I do it again? Honestly, probably not, unless one of my kids begged me. But I think everyone should do it once, as long as you walk in knowing it's hours in a small boat, deep in open water, and it is not for the faint of stomach. If you get seasick, tell me before you book and we'll prepare for it.

## **Bioluminescent Kayaking: The One That Surprised Me**

I loved this one with my whole science-nerd heart. On the right nights, the plankton in the water lights up blue when you move through it. It depends entirely on the moon and tides, so our pickup wasn't until 10:20 at night.

You paddle out to a sandbar, hop into knee-deep water, and they build a little tent to block the light so you can see the glow. Note for when you go: you cannot capture this on your phone. The dreamy blue videos you've seen were shot with special lenses. So leave the phone in the dry bag and just be there. That's the whole point of a place like this anyway.

## **Who I'd Send Here, and Who I Wouldn't**

Isla Holbox is fly-and-flop. You come to be still, to eat well, to meet people from everywhere. On one small island we met travelers from Germany, the Netherlands, Scotland, and Argentina. I never once felt unsafe, walking alone or with my travel partner, day or night. The locals are warm and genuinely happy you came.

I'll be straight about the fit, because that's my whole job. If you want a plan for every hour, big nightlife, and chicken tenders on demand, this is not your island, and I'll point you somewhere better. But if you want a honeymoon or a couples trip that feels real instead of packaged, a place where you wander a town of taco stands and coffee shops and take a 30-minute beachside massage for about 35 dollars, this is one of the best I've found.

## **What I Took Away From Scouting This One**

I only recommend what I've vetted. I got seasick for you. I slept in both hotels for you. I can tell you which side of the island to book and which excursion to think twice about, because I did all of it myself.

Match the traveler to the place. Holbox is a dream for honeymooners, couples, and small groups who want low-key and authentic. It's the wrong call for someone who needs nonstop entertainment, and knowing that difference is what I'm here for.

And if you take one small tip from me: when you travel, go into the local grocery store. I always do. It tells you what a place actually cares about. Holbox even had Dr. Pepper, which almost never happens in Mexico, and yes, I bought some.
